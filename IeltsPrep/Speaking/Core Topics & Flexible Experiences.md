# IELTS Speaking — Core Topics & Flexible Experiences

## 🎯 Main Goal

The goal is **not to memorise hundreds of IELTS Speaking answers**.

Instead, I will prepare a limited number of **core topics, personal experiences, vocabulary sets, examples, and opinions** that can be adapted to many different IELTS Speaking questions.

### The strategy

> **Core Topic → Ideas → Personal Experience → Example → Opinion → Flexible Answer**

# 🎯 The Adaptability Method

When you receive an unfamiliar question, do NOT panic.

Follow these steps:

### Step 1 — Identify the topic

Ask yourself:

> "Which core topic does this question belong to?"

Examples:

* Smartphone → Technology
* Restaurant → Food
* Teacher → Education
* Holiday → Travel
* Exercise → Health
* Childhood friend → Family & Friends
* Dream job → Work & Career

---

### Step 2 — Find a personal experience

Ask:

> "What experience from my life can I connect to this question?"

---

### Step 3 — Give a reason

Use:

> "The main reason is that..."

---

### Step 4 — Give an example

Use:

> "For example, in my own experience..."

---

### Step 5 — Explain your feeling/opinion

Use:

> "Personally, I believe..."

---

### Step 6 — Extend the answer

Use:

> "Another point worth mentioning is that..."

---

# 🗣️ Universal Speaking Framework

## Part 1

Use:

> **Answer → Reason → Example**

### Example

**Question:** Do you like travelling?

> Yes, absolutely. I really enjoy travelling because it gives me an opportunity to escape from my daily routine and experience different cultures. For example, I particularly enjoyed a trip I took with my friends because we explored several beautiful places and tried local food.

---

# Part 2

Use:

> **What → When/Where → Who → What happened → Details → Feelings → Why memorable**

### Framework

> I'd like to talk about **[topic]**.
>
> It happened **[when/where]**.
>
> I was with **[person/people]**.
>
> The main reason I **[did/experienced it]** was that **[reason]**.
>
> What happened was that **[main story]**.
>
> One thing I particularly remember was **[specific detail]**.
>
> At first, I felt **[feeling]**, but later I became **[feeling]**.
>
> Overall, it was a memorable experience because **[reason]**.

---

# Part 3

Use:

> **Opinion → Reason → Explanation → Example → Result**

### Framework

> Personally, I think **[opinion]**.
>
> The main reason is that **[reason]**.
>
> This is mainly because **[explanation]**.
>
> For example, **[example]**.
>
> As a result, **[consequence]**.

---

# 🔗 Linking Words for Speaking

## Adding ideas

* Also
* Besides
* In addition
* Another point is that
* On top of that

## Giving reasons

* Because
* Since
* The main reason is that
* This is mainly because
* One explanation is that

## Giving examples

* For example
* For instance
* To give you an example
* In my own experience

## Contrasting

* However
* Although
* On the other hand
* Having said that
* At the same time

## Giving results

* Therefore
* As a result
* Consequently
* This means that

## Giving opinions

* Personally, I think...
* In my view...
* From my perspective...
* As far as I am concerned...
* I would say that...

# 🏆 Final Strategy

Do not try to memorise:

> ❌ 100 Part 1 answers
> ❌ 100 Part 2 answers
> ❌ 100 Part 3 answers

Instead, build:

> ✅ 15 core topics
> ✅ 10 personal experiences
> ✅ Useful vocabulary
> ✅ Flexible examples
> ✅ Reasons and opinions
> ✅ Natural linking phrases
> ✅ Strong sentence structures

The ultimate goal is:

> **Question → Identify Topic → Choose Experience → Give Reason → Give Example → Explain → Extend**

If you practise this method consistently, unfamiliar questions will become much easier because you will already have **ideas and language that can be adapted to them**.
