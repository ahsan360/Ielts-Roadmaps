# IELTS Speaking Part 2: A Special Cake You Received

## Cue Card

### Describe a special cake you received from someone else.

You should say:

- When it happened
- Where it happened
- Who you got the cake from
- And explain why it was a special cake

---

## A — Answer

I'd like to talk about **a special birthday cake that I received from one of my close friends**. It happened about two years ago on my birthday. I was at home with my family, and I wasn't expecting any special celebration because I had been quite busy with work.

---

## A — Add Details

The cake was actually quite simple, but it looked beautiful. It was a chocolate cake with my name written on it and a small birthday message on top. My friend had chosen it especially for me because he knew that chocolate cake was my favourite.

We had dinner together before cutting the cake. After that, we took some photos, talked about different things, and had a really enjoyable evening. I remember that the atmosphere was very warm and friendly.

---

## A — Anecdote

I particularly remember the moment when my friend suddenly arrived at my house with the cake. I was completely surprised because I hadn't told him that I was celebrating my birthday.

When he gave me the cake, I was genuinely touched. I thanked him and told him that he had made my day much more special.

What made me laugh was that he had actually chosen the wrong flavour at first, but later changed it after remembering that I preferred chocolate. That's a small thing, but I found it quite funny and memorable.

---

## Why It Was Special

I think the cake was special **not because it was expensive or luxurious, but because of the thought behind it**. My friend remembered my birthday, chose something I liked, and made an effort to surprise me.

So, overall, **it was a simple chocolate cake, but it became a very memorable gift because it showed me that my friend genuinely cared about me**.

---

# Part 3: Food and Meals

## 1. What do you think of people using their mobile phones during meals?

Personally, I don't think it's a good habit, especially when people are eating with their family or friends. It can make conversations less meaningful because everyone becomes focused on their phones. However, using a phone briefly for an important message is understandable.

---

## 2. What are the differences between special food in India and other countries?

I think the main difference is the ingredients, cooking methods, and cultural traditions. Indian food is often rich in spices and has a wide variety of vegetarian and non-vegetarian dishes. Other countries have their own special ingredients and cooking styles based on their culture and history.

---

## 3. Is there any food in your country that is eaten on special occasions?

Yes, definitely. In Bangladesh, biryani, polao, and various traditional sweets are commonly served on special occasions. For example, during weddings and religious festivals, families often prepare elaborate meals for their relatives and guests.

---

## 4. Why are some people willing to spend a lot of money on meals on special days?

I think special days are usually about creating memorable experiences rather than simply eating. People may want to celebrate with high-quality food, invite their family and friends, or visit an expensive restaurant. They are willing to spend more because the occasion feels important and happens only occasionally.

---

## 5. Do you think it is good to communicate when eating with your family?

Yes, absolutely. I think family meals are an excellent opportunity to communicate and strengthen relationships. People can talk about their day, share their experiences, and discuss important matters. In my view, this is much healthier than everyone sitting together while using their phones.

---
