# Question 1

## Why do people like to keep pets?

### 💡 Ideas

- Companionship
- Reduce loneliness
- Emotional support
- Security

### 🧠 Band 8 Vocabulary

- companionship
- emotional well-being
- unconditional affection
- stress relief

### 🗣️ Sample Answer

People keep pets for many reasons, but companionship is probably the most important one. Pets provide emotional support, reduce feelings of loneliness, and can even improve mental health by helping people cope with stress. In addition, some animals, such as dogs, offer protection and encourage their owners to lead a more active lifestyle through regular walks.

# Question 2

## What should we do to protect endangered animals?

### 💡 Ideas

- Stronger laws
- Stop poaching
- Protect forests
- Public awareness

### 🧠 Band 8 Vocabulary

- wildlife conservation
- habitat preservation
- strict legislation
- environmental awareness

### 🗣️ Sample Answer

Protecting endangered species requires a combination of government action and public participation. Authorities should enforce stricter laws against poaching and illegal wildlife trade while investing in wildlife reserves and habitat restoration. At the same time, schools and the media should educate people about the importance of biodiversity so that everyone contributes to conservation efforts.

# Question 3

## Do you support doing experiments on animals?

### 💡 Ideas

- Only if necessary
- Medical research
- Alternatives
- Ethical concerns

### 🧠 Band 8 Vocabulary

- ethical concerns
- medical breakthroughs
- alternative methods
- scientific research

### 🗣️ Sample Answer

I think animal testing should only be carried out when there is no practical alternative, particularly in life-saving medical research. Although such research has contributed to important medical breakthroughs, it also raises serious ethical concerns. Therefore, scientists should use modern alternatives, such as computer simulations or laboratory-grown tissues, whenever possible to minimise animal suffering.

# Question 5

## What would happen if some species disappeared from the Earth?

### 💡 Ideas

- Ecosystem imbalance
- Food chain
- Biodiversity loss

### 🧠 Band 8 Vocabulary

- ecological imbalance
- food chain
- irreversible consequences
- biodiversity loss

### 🗣️ Sample Answer

The disappearance of even a single species can disrupt the entire ecosystem because every organism plays a role in maintaining ecological balance. For example, if predators disappear, the populations of other animals may increase uncontrollably, affecting vegetation and other wildlife. In the long run, biodiversity loss could have irreversible consequences for both nature and human life.

# Question 8

## A lot of animals are becoming extinct these days. What do you think about this? Why is it happening?

### 💡 Ideas

- Human activities
- Deforestation
- Pollution
- Climate change

### 🧠 Band 8 Vocabulary

- habitat destruction
- climate change
- illegal hunting
- environmental degradation

### 🗣️ Sample Answer

I believe the increasing extinction of animal species is one of the most serious environmental issues we face today. The main causes are habitat destruction, climate change, pollution, and illegal hunting. As forests are cleared for agriculture and urban development, many animals lose their natural homes. Unless governments and individuals take immediate action, the problem is likely to become even worse.

# Question 10

## Research is being conducted on animals. Is this a good idea?

### 💡 Ideas

- Sometimes necessary
- Ethics
- Alternatives

### 🧠 Band 8 Vocabulary

- scientific progress
- ethical responsibility
- laboratory testing
- humane treatment

### 🗣️ Sample Answer

I think it depends on the purpose of the research. If animal testing is genuinely necessary to develop life-saving medicines and there are no effective alternatives, it can be justified under strict ethical regulations. However, unnecessary testing for cosmetic products or non-essential purposes should be prohibited. Scientists should always prioritise humane treatment and invest in alternative research methods.

# Examiner's Tips (Band 8+)

✔ Give **balanced opinions** on controversial topics such as animal testing.

✔ Use **cause-and-effect language** when discussing extinction.

✔ Support ideas with **examples** rather than general statements.

✔ Use topic-specific vocabulary such as **biodiversity**, **ecosystem**, **habitat destruction**, and **wildlife conservation**.

✔ End with a brief conclusion to make your answer sound complete.
