# Task 1 — Academic

## 1. Introducing the Visual

### Structure

> The chart illustrates + what + place/time.

### Example

> The line graph illustrates changes in the number of people who travelled by different forms of transport between 2000 and 2020.

### Alternative

> The chart provides information about + noun.

---

## 2. Giving the Overview

### Structure

> Overall, it is clear that + main trend.

### Example

> Overall, it is clear that the number of people using public transport increased considerably, whereas car usage declined.

### Alternative

> Overall, X experienced an upward trend, while Y followed a downward trajectory.

### Important

For Task 1, the overview should normally mention:

- The most significant trend
- The highest/lowest figure
- Major differences
- Important changes

---

## 3. Describing an Increase

### Structure

> X increased from A to B between YEAR 1 and YEAR 2.

### Example

> The percentage of people using bicycles increased from 20% to 45% between 2000 and 2020.

### Advanced Structure

> X rose from A to B, representing a significant increase of C.

### Useful Vocabulary

- increased
- rose
- grew
- climbed
- went up
- experienced an upward trend

---

## 4. Describing a Decrease

### Structure

> X decreased from A to B between YEAR 1 and YEAR 2.

### Example

> The proportion of people travelling by car decreased from 60% to 40% over the period.

### Advanced Structure

> X fell from A to B, representing a substantial decline of C.

### Useful Vocabulary

- decreased
- declined
- fell
- dropped
- diminished
- experienced a downward trend

---

## 5. Describing Fluctuation

### Structure

> X fluctuated between A and B throughout the period.

### Example

> The number of visitors fluctuated between 20,000 and 30,000 throughout the period.

### Advanced Structure

> X experienced considerable fluctuations, despite ending the period at a similar level to where it began.

---

## 6. Comparing Two Figures

### Structure

> While X stood at A, Y was considerably higher/lower at B.

### Example

> While the figure for cars stood at 60%, the corresponding figure for bicycles was considerably lower at 20%.

### Alternative

> X was approximately twice as high as Y.

### Example

> The figure for private vehicles was approximately twice as high as that for public transport.

---

## 7. Describing a Peak

### Structure

> X reached a peak of A in YEAR.

### Example

> The number of tourists reached a peak of 2 million in 2019.

### Alternative

> X peaked at A in YEAR.

### Example

> Tourist numbers peaked at 2 million in 2019.

---

## 8. Describing a Difference

### Structure

> There was a difference of A between X and Y.

### Example

> There was a difference of 25 percentage points between the two figures.

### Advanced Structure

> X was A percentage points higher than Y.

### Example

> The proportion of students studying science was 15 percentage points higher than that of students studying history.

---

## 9. Describing Similarities

### Structure

> Both X and Y followed a similar pattern.

### Example

> Both categories followed a similar upward trend throughout the period.

### Alternative

> X and Y were broadly comparable, with figures of A and B respectively.

---

## 10. Describing a Process or Map

### Process Structure

> The process begins with X and ends with Y.

### Example

> The process begins with the collection of raw materials and ends with the distribution of the finished product.

### Sequencing

> Initially, X is + past participle.

> Subsequently, X is + past participle.

> Following this, X is + past participle.

> Finally, X is + past participle.

### Map Structure

> The most noticeable change is that X was replaced by Y.

### Example

> The most noticeable change is that the old car park was replaced by a new shopping centre.

# Task 2 — Essay

## 1. Giving Your Opinion

### Structure

> I firmly believe that + clause.

### Example

> I firmly believe that governments should invest more heavily in public transportation.

### Alternative

> In my view, + clause.

---

## 2. Introducing a Main Reason

### Structure

> One of the primary reasons why X is that Y.

### Example

> One of the primary reasons why people move to large cities is that urban areas provide better employment opportunities.

---

## 3. Explaining an Idea

### Structure

> This is largely because + clause.

### Example

> Online education has become increasingly popular. This is largely because it provides greater flexibility for students.

---

## 4. Giving an Example

### Structure

> A clear example of this can be seen in + noun.

### Example

> A clear example of this can be seen in the increasing number of employees who work remotely.

### Alternative

> For instance, + sentence.

---

## 5. Showing a Consequence

### Structure

> As a result, + consequence.

### Example

> Many people rely heavily on private cars. As a result, urban areas experience severe traffic congestion.

### Advanced Alternative

> This, in turn, can lead to + noun.

### Example

> Excessive screen time can reduce physical activity. This, in turn, can lead to various health problems.

---

## 6. Presenting an Opposing View

### Structure

> Admittedly, X. However, Y.

### Example

> Admittedly, working from home can reduce social interaction. However, its advantages in terms of flexibility and productivity are considerably greater.

---

## 7. Comparing Two Ideas

### Structure

> While X, Y.

### Example

> While some people believe that children should be educated at home, others argue that schools provide a more appropriate environment for social development.

---

## 8. Giving a Conditional Solution

### Structure

> If X, Y could/would + verb.

### Example

> If governments introduced stricter regulations, they could significantly reduce the amount of plastic waste produced.

---

## 9. Showing Two Effects

### Structure

> Not only does X + verb, but it also + verb.

### Example

> Not only does public transportation reduce traffic congestion, but it also helps to lower carbon emissions.

### Grammar Warning

Correct:

> Not only does it reduce pollution, but it also improves public health.

Incorrect:

> Not only it reduces pollution, but it also improves public health.

---

## 10. Writing a Strong Conclusion

### Structure

> Taking these factors into consideration, it is clear that + opinion.

### Example

> Taking these factors into consideration, it is clear that governments should prioritise investment in public transportation rather than expanding road networks.

# ⭐ IELTS Strategy

## Task 1

Focus on:

1. **Paraphrasing the question**
2. **Writing a clear overview**
3. **Selecting important data**
4. **Making comparisons**
5. **Using accurate numerical language**
6. **Using a mixture of simple and complex sentences**

## Task 2

Focus on:

1. **Answering the question directly**
2. **Developing each main idea**
3. **Explaining why**
4. **Giving relevant examples**
5. **Showing consequences**
6. **Using complex sentences accurately**
7. **Maintaining logical progression**
8. **Writing a clear conclusion**

> **Golden rule:** Do not try to insert every structure into one essay. Choose structures that naturally fit your ideas. IELTS rewards **flexibility, accuracy and coherence**, rather than memorised language.
