## 1. Household Spending across Four Countries (2009)

### Question
The bar chart below shows shares of expenditures for five major categories in the United States, Canada, the United Kingdom, and Japan in the year 2009.

### Sample Answer
The bar chart compares the proportion of household expenditure spent across five different categories in four countries in 2009.

Overall, housing accounted for the highest share of total expenditure in all four nations. In contrast, healthcare and clothing consistently represented the lowest spending categories for every country.

Regarding housing, the United States recorded the largest proportion of spending at approximately 26%. The United Kingdom followed closely at 24%, while Canada and Japan allocated around 21% and 22% respectively. For food, Japan spent the highest share at 23%, followed by the United Kingdom at 20%. Conversely, households in Canada and the United States spent the least on food, at 15% and 14% respectively.

In terms of transportation, Canada recorded the highest expenditure at 20%, whereas the United States and the United Kingdom spent 17% and 15% respectively. Japan allocated the smallest share to transportation at 10%. For healthcare, the United States led at 7%, while the other three countries spent under 5% each. Finally, spending on clothing remained relatively uniform, accounting for around 4% to 6% across all four nations.

## 2. Community Website Visits (First vs Second Year)

### Question
The bar chart below shows the number of visits to a community website in the first and second years of use.

### Sample Answer
The bar chart compares the monthly number of visits to a community website during its first and second years of operation.

Overall, the website recorded significantly higher traffic throughout the second year compared to the first year. Additionally, while traffic in the second year showed large fluctuations, first-year visits remained comparatively low until a notable rise in the summer months.

In the second year, website visits started at around 3,000 in September and increased steadily to approximately 18,000 in November and December. After dipping to 10,000 between February and March, traffic climbed sharply, peaking at over 22,000 visits in both June and July. However, traffic dropped significantly to around 12,500 visits in August.

In the first year, traffic began at under 2,000 visits in September before rising to a local peak of 10,000 in November and December. Visits then dropped sharply, remaining below 3,000 between January and March. From April onward, monthly visits increased gradually, reaching a peak of 15,000 in August, which was the only month where first-year traffic surpassed second-year traffic.

## 3. Unemployment Rates for Elderly People (1980 vs 2000)

### Question
The bar charts compare the percentage of unemployed men and women over the age of 60 in Belgium, the USA, Japan, and Australia in 1980 and 2000.

### Sample Answer
The bar charts compare the unemployment rates among men and women aged over 60 across four countries in 1980 and 2000.

Overall, unemployment rates were substantially higher for elderly men than for elderly women in both years. Furthermore, while male unemployment decreased in all four countries over the 20-year period, female unemployment showed mixed trends.

Among men aged over 60, Japan recorded the highest unemployment rate in 1980 at 89%, followed by Belgium at 83%, the USA at 78%, and Australia at 75%. By 2000, male unemployment had decreased across all nations. Japan remained the highest at 72%, while the figures for the USA and Australia fell to 65% and 47% respectively. Belgium experienced the largest decline, dropping sharply to 20%.

Among women, Japan again registered the highest unemployment rate in both years, despite dropping from 53% in 1980 to 48% in 2000. In contrast, female unemployment increased in the USA (from 36% to 40%) and Australia (from 18% to 21%). Belgium recorded the lowest female unemployment rate, falling from 8% in 1980 to 5% in 2000.

## 4. Household Spending Patterns by Income Group

### Question
The bar chart shows the average household spending pattern for households in three income categories as a proportion of their income.

### Sample Answer
The bar chart compares the proportion of income spent on ten different categories by households across three income brackets: low, medium, and high.

Overall, housing and transport represented the largest expenditure categories across all income levels. In contrast, education, entertainment, and eating out accounted for the smallest shares of income.

Low- and medium-income households allocated the highest proportion of their earnings to housing, at nearly 29% each, compared to around 28% for high-income earners. Transport was the second largest expense, taking nearly 19% of medium-income earnings, 18% of low-income earnings, and 15% of high-income earnings. Utilities and food at home accounted for larger shares of low-income budgets (13% and 12% respectively) compared to high-income budgets (5% and 6%).

In terms of savings and discretionary spending, high-income households saved 17% of their income, whereas low-income households saved only 3%. High-income households also spent larger proportions on education (5%) and entertainment (6%) compared to low-income households, which allocated less than 3% to each category.
