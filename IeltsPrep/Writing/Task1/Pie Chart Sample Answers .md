## 1. UK Household Spending (1971 vs 2001)

### Question
The pie charts show changes in the spending habits of people in the UK between 1971 and 2001.

### Sample Answer
The pie charts illustrate how people in the UK allocated their expenditure across seven different categories in 1971 and 2001.

Overall, it is clear that food accounted for the largest share of spending in 1971, whereas cars became the primary expense by 2001. Additionally, expenditure on computers and dining out saw notable increases, while spending on food dropped significantly.

In 1971, British households spent the majority of their income on food, at 44%. Cars represented the second largest category at 22%, followed by petrol at 10% and furniture at 9%. Restaurants, books, and computers made up smaller proportions, accounting for 7%, 6%, and 2% respectively.

By 2001, expenditure patterns had shifted considerably. Spending on cars nearly doubled to become the largest single category at 43%. Similarly, the proportion spent on restaurants doubled to 14%, while spending on computers jumped sixfold to 12%. Conversely, the share of spending on food fell dramatically to 14%. Expenditure on petrol and furniture decreased slightly to 8% each, while book purchases dropped to just 1%.

## 2. Ocean Pollutants (1997 vs 2007)

### Question
The two pie charts show the percentage of pollutants entering a particular part of the ocean in 1997 and 2007.

### Sample Answer
The pie charts compare the main sources of pollution in a specific area of the ocean in two different years, 1997 and 2007.

Overall, it is evident that pollutants from the air represented the single largest source of ocean contamination in both years. Furthermore, domestic sewage and offshore oil pollution increased notably over the ten-year period, while industrial waste and litter declined.

In 1997, air pollutants formed the largest share of total pollution at 30%, followed by marine pollution at 22% and domestic sewage at 18%. Industrial waste contributed 17%, while offshore oil, farm waste, and litter accounted for much smaller shares at 5%, 4%, and 4% respectively.

By 2007, domestic sewage experienced a significant rise, reaching 29% of the total. Similarly, offshore oil pollution nearly tripled to 14%. In contrast, air pollutants remained almost unchanged at 29%. On the other hand, marine pollution dropped to 17%, and industrial waste nearly halved to 9%. Farm waste decreased to 2%, while litter was entirely eliminated, falling to 0%.

## 3. Children's Leisure Activities (Boys vs Girls)

### Question
The pie charts compare the percentage of boys and girls participating in different cultural and leisure activities.

### Sample Answer
The pie charts illustrate the proportion of boys and girls participating in various cultural and leisure activities.

Overall, it is clear that boys spent the majority of their time on screen-based entertainment and outdoor sports. In contrast, girls showed a stronger preference for creative arts and reading.

Among boys, playing computer games was the most popular activity, accounting for 34% of total participation. Basketball was the second most common choice at 26%, followed by soccer at 17% and skateboarding at 11%. Music attracted 10% of boys, while reading recorded the lowest participation rate at only 2%.

Among girls, dancing emerged as the top activity, representing 27% of overall engagement. Reading was also popular among females at 21%, which was far higher than the figure for boys. Computer games accounted for 16% of girls' participation, followed by netball at 15% and gymnastics at 11%. Listening to music accounted for 10%, showing identical popularity among both genders.

## 4. Energy Production Sources (1985 vs 2003)

### Question
The pie charts show the proportion of energy produced from different sources in a country between 1985 and 2003.

### Sample Answer
The pie charts compare the different sources used to generate energy in a specific nation in 1985 and 2003.

Overall, it is evident that oil remained the primary source of energy generation in both years, despite a notable drop in its share. Conversely, natural gas and coal witnessed clear increases over the 18-year period.

In 1985, oil dominated the country's energy production, supplying over half of the total at 52%. Nuclear power was the second largest contributor at 22%, followed by natural gas at 13% and coal at 8%. Energy produced from hydrogen and other renewable resources made up the remaining share, at 4% and 1% respectively.

By 2003, the proportion of energy generated from oil fell to 39%, though it remained the leading source. Nuclear energy also decreased to 17%. In contrast, natural gas experienced significant growth, rising to 23%, while coal production grew to 13%. Hydrogen remained constant at 4%, whereas other renewable sources rose slightly to 4%.

## 5. Global Forest Cover and Timber Resources

### Question
The pie charts show the percentage of world forest area and timber resources across five different regions.

### Sample Answer
The pie charts compare the distribution of global forest coverage and timber resources across five major regions worldwide.

Overall, it is clear that Africa holds the largest share of the world's forests, whereas North America possesses the greatest proportion of global timber. Additionally, there is a clear difference between forest coverage and timber yield in several regions.

Regarding forest coverage, Africa accounts for the largest proportion at 27%, followed closely by North America at 25%. Europe comprises 20% of global forest land, while South America and Asia hold equal shares of 15% each.

In terms of timber resources, North America dominates the global share at 30%. South America and Europe follow with 23% and 20% respectively. Asia supplies 18% of the world's timber. In contrast, Africa accounts for the smallest share of global timber at only 9%, despite having the largest area of forest land.

## 6. Dangerous Waste Management (Korea, Sweden, UK)

### Question
The pie charts show how dangerous waste products are managed in three countries: Republic of Korea, Sweden, and the United Kingdom.

### Sample Answer
The pie charts compare the various methods utilized to handle hazardous waste materials in three distinct countries: the Republic of Korea, Sweden, and the United Kingdom.

Overall, it is noticeable that the United Kingdom and Sweden rely predominantly on underground burial to dispose of dangerous waste. In contrast, recycling is the primary method employed by the Republic of Korea.

In the Republic of Korea, recycling accounts for the vast majority of hazardous waste management at 69%. Underground burial handles 22% of the total waste, while incineration represents the remaining 9%.

In Sweden, underground disposal is the most common technique, processing 55% of all dangerous waste. Recycling represents 25%, while incineration handles 20%. In the UK, underground burial is even more dominant, accounting for 82% of total waste. Chemical treatment and sea dumping handle 8% each, while incineration accounts for a minor 2%.

## 7. TV Program Viewership by Age Group

### Question
The pie charts show the TV program preferences among three different age groups (10-15, 16-20, and 21-25 years old).

### Sample Answer
The pie charts compare the TV viewing choices of individuals across three age brackets: 10 to 15 years, 16 to 20 years, and 21 to 25 years.

Overall, it is evident that sports and cartoons are the most favored programs among younger viewers, whereas drama appeals most to teenagers. In contrast, news programs are the dominant preference among young adults.

For the 10–15 age group, cartoons and sports are the most popular genres, each capturing 26% of viewership. Drama follows closely at 23%, while films attract 19%. News represents the least watched category at just 6%.

For the 16–20 age group, drama is the leading genre at 30%, followed by films at 23%. News, cartoons, and sports account for smaller proportions at 18%, 15%, and 14% respectively.

For the 21–25 age group, news becomes the most popular category, capturing 33% of viewers. Drama accounts for 22%, films for 21%, and cartoons for 20%. Sports programs represent the least popular genre for this age group, at only 4%.

## 8. Homework 1: Global Food Consumption (World, China, India)

### Question
The pie charts show the average consumption of food in the world in 2008 compared to two countries: China and India.

### Sample Answer
The pie charts compare the average food consumption patterns globally with those of China and India in 2008 across four key food categories.

Overall, processed food accounted for the largest share of food intake globally and in India, whereas vegetables and fruits represented the most consumed category in China. In addition, nuts and seeds formed the smallest portion across all three charts.

Globally, processed food made up the highest proportion of dietary intake at 41%, followed by vegetables and fruits at 29%, and animal food at 26%. Nuts and seeds represented a minor 4% of total worldwide consumption.

In China, vegetables and fruits formed the primary category at 32%, closely followed by processed food at 34%. Nuts and seeds accounted for a significant 19%, while animal food made up 15%. In India, processed food was the largest category at 39%, while animal food accounted for 27%. Vegetables and fruits comprised 23%, and nuts and seeds made up 11%.

## 9. Homework 2: Native Languages at Bakersfield Senior College (1980 vs 1990)

### Question
The pie chart below shows the native languages spoken by students at Bakersfield Senior College in 1980 and 1990.

### Sample Answer
The pie charts compare the primary native languages spoken by students at Bakersfield Senior College in 1980 and 1990.

Overall, English was the dominant native language spoken by the student body in both years, despite a notable decline over the decade. Conversely, the proportion of students speaking Chinese and other non-listed languages showed significant growth.

In 1980, English native speakers constituted the vast majority of the student population at 70%. Chinese was the second most common native language at 15%, followed by Urdu at 9%. Other languages accounted for the remaining 6%.

By 1990, the proportion of native English speakers dropped significantly to 55%, though it remained the largest group. In contrast, Chinese native speakers nearly doubled to 28%. Students speaking other languages also increased, reaching 11%. Meanwhile, the percentage of native Urdu speakers declined slightly to 6%.
