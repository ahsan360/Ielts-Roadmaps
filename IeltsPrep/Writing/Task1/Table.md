## Task 1: Postgraduate Course Fees (Table 1)

### Question
The table below shows the postgraduate course fees that international students paid in three countries in 2007. Summarise the information by selecting and reporting the main features, and make comparisons where relevant.

| Field / Area | Country A | Country B | Country C |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Arts** | $8,000 | $18,000 | $19,000 |
| **Commerce** | $8,000 | $18,000 | $19,000 |
| **Computing** | $11,000 | $27,000 | $24,000 |
| **Science** | $11,000 | $29,000 | $25,000 |
| **Meals and Accommodation** | $8,000 | $17,000 | $11,000 |

### Model Answer (Band 6.5–7.0)

The table compares postgraduate tuition fees across four academic fields, along with living costs for international students in three different countries in 2007.

Overall, what stands out from the table is that Country B and Country C charged significantly higher tuition and living fees than Country A across all categories. Furthermore, Science and Computing were consistently the most expensive academic subjects in all three nations, whereas Arts and Commerce were the least expensive.

In terms of academic courses, Country B recorded the highest tuition fee for Science at $29,000, closely followed by Country C at $25,000 and Country A at $11,000. For Computing, Country B was again the most expensive at $27,000, while Country C charged $24,000 and Country A charged $11,000. Conversely, Arts and Commerce cost identical amounts within each country, with Country C charging the most at $19,000, followed by Country B at $18,000, and Country A at $8,000.

Regarding living costs, Country B was the most expensive destination for meals and accommodation, requiring international students to pay $17,000. In comparison, students in Country C paid $11,000, while Country A offered the lowest living costs at $8,000.

## Task 2: Temporary Migrant Workers (Table 2)

### Question
The table below gives information on temporary migrant workers in four countries in 2003 and 2006. Summarise the information by selecting and reporting the main features, and make comparisons where relevant.

| Country | 2003 | 2006 | Migrants per 1,000 population (2006) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **UK** | 100,326 | 120,780 | 18.3 |
| **Australia** | 801,250 | 920,470 | 16.8 |
| **New Zealand** | 60,520 | 80,620 | 21.4 |
| **USA** | 900,325 | 1,000,240 | 19.6 |

### Model Answer (Band 6.5–7.0)

The table illustrates data regarding the total numbers of temporary foreign workers in four selected countries in 2003 and 2006, as well as the proportion of migrants per 1,000 residents.

Overall, what stands out from the table is that all four nations experienced an upward trend in temporary migrant worker numbers over the three-year period. Additionally, while the USA hosted the largest absolute number of migrant workers, New Zealand registered the highest ratio of migrants relative to its total population.

In terms of total figures, the USA recorded the highest number of temporary foreign workers in both years, starting at 900,325 in 2003 and rising to 1,000,240 in 2006. Australia had the second-largest totals, growing from 801,250 to 920,470 over the same timeframe. By contrast, the UK and New Zealand registered much lower absolute figures, with the UK increasing from 100,326 to 120,780 and New Zealand rising from 60,520 to 80,620.

Regarding the migrant-to-population ratio, New Zealand led the group with 21.4 temporary workers per 1,000 residents. The USA followed closely with 19.6, while the UK stood at 18.3. Australia recorded the lowest proportion among the four countries, with 16.8 migrants per 1,000 people.

## Task 3: Proportion of Income Spent on 4 Items (Table 3)

### Question
The chart below shows the proportion of income adults and children spent on 4 common items in the UK in 1998. Summarise the information by selecting and reporting the main features, and make comparisons where relevant.

| Group | Food | Electronic Equipment | Music | Videos |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Adults (Total)** | 25% | 5% | 5% | 1% |
| **Men** | 14% | 10% | 5% | 2% |
| **Women** | 39% | 1% | 5% | 0.5% |
| **Children (Total)** | 10% | 23% | 39% | 12% |
| **Boys** | 9% | 18% | 38% | 18% |
| **Girls** | 11% | 5% | 40% | 17% |

### Model Answer (Band 6.5–7.0)

The table compares the proportion of income allocated to four different consumer items—food, electronic equipment, music, and videos—by adults and children in the UK in 1998.

Overall, adults spent the largest share of their earnings on food, whereas children devoted the vast majority of their budget to music and electronic equipment. Conversely, spending on videos remained low across almost all groups.

Looking at food expenditure, women allocated the highest percentage of their income to this category at 39%, which contributed to an overall adult average of 25%. In contrast, men spent only 14% on food. Children spent far less on food, with total expenditure at 10% (9% for boys and 11% for girls). 

Regarding leisure items, children directed 39% of their spending toward music, led by girls at 40% and boys at 38%. In contrast, all adult groups spent a minor 5% on music. However, children allocated 23% of their budget to electronic devices—primarily driven by boys at 18% compared to girls at 5%—while adult electronics spending remained at or below 10%. Finally, video expenditure was highest among boys (18%) and girls (17%), whereas women spent the smallest fraction at just 0.5%.

## Task 4: World CO2 Emissions (Table 4)

### Question
The table below shows CO2 emissions from 4 regions of the world and total emissions between 1995 and 2015. Summarise the information by selecting and reporting the main features, and make comparisons where relevant.

| Region | 1995 | 2000 | 2005 | 2010 | 2015 |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Africa** | 1.0 | 1.3 | 1.5 | 1.7 | 1.9 |
| **USA** | 4.6 | 4.3 | 4.7 | 5.0 | 5.2 |
| **Asia** | 5.0 | 7.2 | 10.3 | 12.0 | 14.0 |
| **Europe** | 7.3 | 7.5 | 6.0 | 5.1 | 4.1 |
| **Total** | 20.0 | 24.0 | 29.0 | 31.0 | 35.0 |

*(Note: Emissions in billion metric tons)*

### Model Answer (Band 6.5–7.0)

The table compares global carbon dioxide ($CO_2$) emissions across four regions, along with total world emissions, over a 20-year period from 1995 to 2015.

Overall, total global $CO_2$ emissions increased significantly over the period. Asia witnessed the most dramatic growth and became the dominant polluter by a wide margin, whereas Europe was the only region to experience an overall decline.

In 1995, Europe was the largest emitter of carbon dioxide at 7.3 billion metric tons, followed by Asia at 5.0 billion and the USA at 4.6 billion. Africa emitted the lowest amount at just 1.0 billion metric tons. By 2015, Europe's emissions fell continuously to 4.1 billion metric tons, making it the second lowest emitter by the end of the timeframe.

In contrast, emissions in Asia surged dramatically, nearly tripling from 5.0 billion in 1995 to 14.0 billion in 2015. The USA and Africa also saw gradual increases, reaching 5.2 billion and 1.9 billion metric tons respectively by 2015. As a consequence of these developments, total global emissions rose steadily from 20.0 billion metric tons in 1995 to 35.0 billion by 2015.
