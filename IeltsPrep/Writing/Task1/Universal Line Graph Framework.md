# STEP 2 — Introduction

## Formula

The line graph illustrates + WHAT + WHERE + WHEN.

Example

The line graph illustrates the number of participants in five activities at a social centre in Melbourne between 2000 and 2020.

Another example

The line graph compares the unemployment rate in three countries from 1990 to 2020.

# STEP 3 — Overview ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

Never use many numbers.

Answer these four questions.

## Which line was highest?

> Overall, X remained the highest throughout the period.

---

## Which line was lowest?

> Y recorded the lowest figure.

---

## Which increased the most?

> Z experienced the most significant growth.

---

## Which decreased?

> A declined steadily over the period.

---

## Universal Overview Template

Overall, **(highest category)** remained the largest/highest throughout most of the period despite **(trend)**. In contrast, **(category)** recorded the most significant increase, whereas **(category)** declined steadily and finished as the lowest category. Meanwhile, **(category)** remained relatively stable/fluctuated slightly.

---

# STEP 4 — Body Paragraph 1

Usually describe

Highest line

+

Second highest line

Template

Initially, **X** stood at approximately **A**, making it the highest figure. It then **rose/fell/fluctuated** to **B** before ending the period at **C**. Similarly, **Y** began at around **D** and **increased/decreased** steadily to **E** by the final year.

---

# STEP 5 — Body Paragraph 2

Describe remaining lines.

Template

By contrast, **Z** started at only **A** before **increasing/decreasing** steadily to **B**. Meanwhile, **W** experienced **a gradual increase / slight fluctuation / dramatic decline**, finishing the period at approximately **C**.

# How to Group Lines

Instead of describing each line separately:

❌

Manufacturing...

Retail...

Healthcare...

Agriculture...

---

Describe similar trends together.

Example

Paragraph 1

Highest + Second Highest

Paragraph 2

Lowest + Fastest Growth

OR

Paragraph 1

Increasing Lines

Paragraph 2

Decreasing/Stable Lines

This improves coherence and cohesion.

# Universal Checklist Before Writing

- What is the graph about?
- What is the time period?
- What unit is used?
- Which line is the highest?
- Which line is the lowest?
- Which line increases the most?
- Which line decreases the most?
- Does any line remain stable?
- Does any line fluctuate?
- Do any lines cross each other?

---

# Universal Band 7+ Checklist

- ✅ Four paragraphs
- ✅ Paraphrased introduction
- ✅ Clear overview
- ✅ Accurate data
- ✅ Compare instead of listing
- ✅ Group similar trends
- ✅ Wide range of vocabulary
- ✅ Correct tense
- ✅ Around 170–190 words
- ✅ No opinions
- ✅ No explanation of reasons
- ✅ Check grammar and spelling

# 20-Minute Writing Plan

| Time | Task |
|------|------|
| 0–2 min | Analyse the graph |
| 2–4 min | Write the Introduction and Overview |
| 4–16 min | Write the two Body Paragraphs |
| 16–20 min | Proofread grammar, spelling, numbers, and word count |

---

# Golden Rule

> **Describe trends, not every data point.**

The examiner wants you to **summarise**, **compare**, and **highlight key features**, rather than narrating every value on the graph.
