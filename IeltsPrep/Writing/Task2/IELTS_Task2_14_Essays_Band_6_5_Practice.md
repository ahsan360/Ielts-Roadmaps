# IELTS Writing Task 2 — 14 Band 6.5 Practice Essays
## A reusable structure with simple vocabulary and reliable complex sentences

**How to use this file**

These are practice models, not essays to memorise word for word. Learn the paragraph structure and sentence patterns, then adapt them to the exact question in the exam.

Each essay follows the same structure:
1. **Introduction:** Paraphrase the topic + give a clear opinion.
2. **Body paragraph 1:** Give the first reason, explain it, and provide an example.
3. **Body paragraph 2:** Give another point or discuss the opposite side.
4. **Conclusion:** Restate your position and summarise the main reasons.

### Reusable complex-sentence patterns

- **Although** [opposing point], I believe that [your opinion] because [reason].
- One important reason is that [main idea].
- **When** [situation], [result].
- People who [verb] are more likely to [verb].
- **For example,** [specific example].
- **As a result,** [consequence].
- **However,** [contrasting idea].
- In conclusion, I believe that [opinion] because [summary].

Use these structures accurately. You do not need to make every sentence complex; a mixture of clear simple sentences and accurate complex sentences is more effective.

# Essay 7 — Communication and Personal Relationships

### Question
Modern technology has made communication faster and easier, but it has reduced the quality of personal relationships. To what extent do you agree or disagree?

### Model answer

Modern technology has changed the way people communicate. Although it allows people to contact one another quickly and easily, I partly agree that it can weaken personal relationships because some people spend too much time on their devices.

One important reason is that technology can reduce face-to-face communication. Many people use their phones even when they are sitting with family members or friends. When people focus on their screens instead of talking to one another, they may miss opportunities to share their feelings and understand each other. For example, family members may sit together at dinner while each person looks at a separate phone. As a result, they may gradually feel less connected.

However, technology can also help people maintain close relationships. Video calls and messaging applications allow people to stay in touch with relatives who live far away. For example, someone who works in another country can speak to their parents regularly through video calls. This can help families remain emotionally close even when they cannot meet in person.

In conclusion, I partly agree that modern technology can harm personal relationships when it replaces face-to-face conversations. However, it can also strengthen relationships by helping people communicate across long distances.

# Essay 8 — Social Media and News

### Question
Social media has become the primary source of news for many people. Do you think this is a positive or negative development?

### Model answer

Social media has become a common way for people to receive news. Although it provides information quickly and conveniently, I believe this is mainly a negative development because false information can spread easily.

One important advantage is that social media allows people to learn about events as they happen. Users can follow news pages and receive updates without waiting for a newspaper or television programme. When an important event takes place, information can reach people within minutes. For example, people may learn about severe weather or traffic problems through local social media pages. As a result, they can respond more quickly to changing situations.

However, social media does not always provide reliable information. Almost anyone can publish a story, even when they have not checked whether it is true. People who share news without checking the source may unintentionally spread false claims. For example, an inaccurate report about a public event can be shared thousands of times before it is corrected. This may confuse people and damage trust.

In conclusion, I believe that relying mainly on social media for news is a negative development. Although it is fast and convenient, people should check important stories against trustworthy news sources before accepting or sharing them.

# Essay 12 — Crime, Poverty and Education

### Question
The increase in crime rates is mainly caused by poverty and lack of education. To what extent do you agree or disagree?

### Model answer

Some people believe that poverty and a lack of education are the main causes of rising crime rates. I largely agree because financial difficulties and limited educational opportunities can make it harder for people to find legal ways to earn a living.

One important reason is that poverty can place people under serious financial pressure. People who struggle to afford food, housing and other basic needs may feel desperate, although most people living in poverty do not commit crimes. When individuals cannot find work or support their families, some may be tempted to earn money illegally. For example, a person who has been unemployed for a long time may turn to theft to pay for basic necessities. As a result, financial hardship can increase the risk of crime in some communities.

A lack of education can also limit employment opportunities. People who do not receive suitable education or training may find it difficult to obtain stable work. For example, young people who leave school without useful skills may struggle to find a job. However, crime can also be influenced by family problems, peer pressure and drug addiction.

In conclusion, I largely agree that poverty and a lack of education contribute to crime. Governments should improve access to education and job training while also addressing other social problems that may lead to criminal behaviour.

# Essay 14 — Police Officers and Firearms

### Question
Police officers should carry firearms at all times to ensure public safety. To what extent do you agree or disagree?

### Model answer

Some people believe that police officers should carry firearms at all times to protect the public. I partly disagree because guns can be necessary in dangerous situations, but carrying them constantly may create additional risks.

One important reason to provide firearms to police officers is that they may face violent criminals. When an officer responds to an armed robbery or another serious threat, a firearm may help protect the officer and members of the public. For example, officers dealing with an armed attacker may need suitable equipment to prevent further injuries. As a result, access to firearms can be important in high-risk situations.

However, not every police duty involves serious violence. Many officers deal with traffic problems, minor disputes and other routine matters. If every officer carries a gun at all times, mistakes or unnecessary use of force could have serious consequences. For example, a tense situation might become more dangerous if a weapon is used when it is not needed. Police training and clear rules are therefore essential.

In conclusion, I do not believe that every police officer must carry a firearm at all times. Although firearms can be necessary when serious threats arise, officers should receive proper training and use weapons only when the situation justifies it.

# Essay 15 — Preserving Historical Buildings

### Question
Historical buildings should be preserved even if it costs a lot of money to maintain them. To what extent do you agree or disagree?

### Model answer

Historical buildings are an important part of a country's heritage. Although maintaining them can be expensive, I largely agree that important historical buildings should be preserved because they teach future generations about the past and can attract tourists.

One important reason is that historical buildings help people understand their culture and history. They show how people lived and how communities developed in earlier times. When children visit these places, they can learn about history in a more direct way than by reading books alone. For example, an old palace or traditional house can show visitors the design and lifestyle of a previous period. As a result, preserving such buildings can help protect a country's cultural identity.

Historical buildings can also support local businesses through tourism. Visitors may spend money on transport, hotels, restaurants and local products. For example, a well-maintained historical site may attract tourists who also visit nearby shops. This can create jobs and provide income for local residents.

However, governments have limited budgets and must also pay for services such as education and healthcare. Therefore, they should give priority to buildings with significant historical or cultural value rather than spending large sums on every old structure.

In conclusion, I largely agree that important historical buildings should be preserved even when maintenance is costly. However, governments should consider each building's value and condition before deciding how much money to spend.

# Essay 24 — Examinations and Academic Performance

### Question
Examinations are the best way to measure a student's academic performance. To what extent do you agree or disagree?

### Model answer

Examinations are widely used to assess students' academic performance. Although they can show how much students have learned, I disagree that they are the best method because they cannot measure every skill that students need.

One important advantage of examinations is that they provide a common way to assess many students. Students can answer similar questions under similar conditions, which makes it easier for teachers to compare their knowledge. For example, a final science examination can show whether students understand the main ideas taught during a course. As a result, examinations can help teachers identify areas in which students need further support.

However, examinations do not measure all abilities. Some students become nervous during tests and perform less well than they normally would. In addition, written examinations may not show how well students work in a team, solve practical problems or communicate with others. For example, a student may be good at programming but find it difficult to explain their skills in a timed written test. Projects and presentations can provide a fuller picture of such abilities.

In conclusion, I disagree that examinations are the best way to measure academic performance. Although they are useful for checking knowledge, schools should also use projects, presentations and practical tasks to assess students more fairly.

# Essay 29 — Celebrities as Role Models

### Question
Celebrities have a responsibility to act as good role models for young people. To what extent do you agree or disagree?

### Model answer

Celebrities can influence the behaviour and opinions of young people. I largely agree that they should act as good role models because their actions may affect their followers, although parents and schools also have an important role in guiding children.

One important reason is that young people often admire famous actors, athletes and online personalities. When celebrities promote healthy habits and respectful behaviour, their followers may be encouraged to do the same. For example, a well-known athlete who speaks about regular exercise may motivate young people to become more active. As a result, celebrities can use their popularity to encourage positive choices.

However, celebrities do not always set a good example. Some may promote dangerous behaviour or make irresponsible comments online. Young people who copy these actions without thinking carefully may develop unhealthy habits. For example, a teenager may believe that risky behaviour is acceptable if a famous person appears to support it. This is why public figures should consider the possible effects of their actions.

Nevertheless, parents and teachers should also help young people understand right and wrong. Children need guidance so that they can think critically rather than copy everything they see.

In conclusion, I largely agree that celebrities should behave responsibly because they can influence young people. However, parents and schools must also help children make sensible decisions.

# Essay 32 — Television and Children

### Question
Watching television is a waste of time for children. To what extent do you agree or disagree?

### Model answer

Some people believe that watching television is a waste of time for children. I partly disagree because watching too much television can be harmful, but suitable programmes can also educate and entertain children.

One important disadvantage is that excessive television viewing can reduce the time children spend on other activities. When children watch television for several hours each day, they may have less time for exercise, reading and homework. For example, a child who watches programmes throughout the evening may not finish schoolwork or get enough sleep. As a result, too much television can affect children's health and learning.

However, television can also provide useful educational content. Some programmes teach children about science, nature, history and other subjects in an interesting way. For example, a documentary about animals may help children learn about wildlife and the environment. Educational programmes can also introduce new words and ideas. Therefore, the effect of television depends partly on what children watch and how much time they spend watching it.

Parents should set reasonable limits and encourage children to take part in other activities as well. This can help them enjoy television without allowing it to dominate their daily routine.

In conclusion, I partly disagree that watching television is a waste of time for children. Although excessive viewing can be harmful, carefully selected programmes can be useful and enjoyable.

# Essay 35 — Plastic Pollution

### Question
Plastic pollution is the most serious environmental problem facing the world today. To what extent do you agree or disagree?

### Model answer

Plastic pollution is a major environmental problem in many parts of the world. Although it causes serious damage to nature, I partly disagree that it is the most serious problem because climate change and deforestation also have wide-ranging effects.

One important reason plastic pollution is dangerous is that plastic waste can remain in the environment for a long time. It often enters rivers and oceans, where animals may swallow it or become trapped in it. For example, sea animals can mistake plastic bags for food, which may injure or kill them. As a result, plastic waste can damage wildlife and pollute natural areas.

However, other environmental problems are also serious. Climate change can lead to higher temperatures, extreme weather and rising sea levels. Deforestation destroys animal habitats and reduces the number of trees that absorb carbon dioxide. For example, communities that depend on farming may suffer when changing weather patterns damage crops. These problems can affect large numbers of people and ecosystems.

Governments should therefore address several environmental threats rather than focusing on plastic alone. They can reduce single-use plastics, protect forests and support cleaner energy sources.

In conclusion, I agree that plastic pollution is a serious global challenge, but I do not think it is necessarily the most serious one. Climate change and deforestation also require urgent attention.

# Essay 39 — Elderly People and Retirement Homes

### Question
Elderly people should live with their family members rather than in retirement homes. To what extent do you agree or disagree?

### Model answer

Some people believe that elderly people should live with their families instead of in retirement homes. I partly agree because living with relatives can provide emotional support, but professional care may be more suitable for some older people.

One important advantage of living with family is that elderly people can spend more time with their children and grandchildren. When older people have regular contact with relatives, they may feel less lonely and more supported. For example, family members can help an elderly parent with shopping, cooking or daily tasks. As a result, living together can strengthen family relationships and improve older people's well-being.

However, some elderly people need specialised care that their families cannot provide. Many adult children work full-time, while some older people have health problems that require trained staff. For example, a person who needs regular medical attention may receive better support in a well-managed care home. Such places may also organise activities that allow residents to socialise with others.

The best choice therefore depends on the older person's health, preferences and family situation. Families should discuss the available options and choose an arrangement that provides safety and respect.

In conclusion, I partly agree that elderly people should live with their families because this can offer companionship and support. However, retirement homes can be appropriate when professional care is needed.

# Essay 45 — Sharing Childcare and Housework

### Question
Both parents should share equal responsibility for childcare and housework. To what extent do you agree or disagree?

### Model answer

Some people believe that both parents should share responsibility for childcare and housework equally. I strongly agree because sharing these duties can reduce stress and create a fairer family life.

One important reason is that managing a home can be difficult when one parent does most of the work. When both parents contribute to cooking, cleaning and childcare, each person has more time to rest and work towards personal goals. For example, one parent can prepare dinner while the other helps the children with their homework. As a result, household duties become more manageable and neither parent has to carry the entire burden.

Sharing responsibilities can also teach children valuable lessons about equality. Children who see both parents doing household tasks may understand that these duties should not be assigned to one gender. For example, a child who regularly sees their father cooking and their mother repairing things may develop a more balanced view of family roles. This can encourage greater fairness in their future relationships.

Of course, parents may need to divide tasks according to their working hours, health and other responsibilities. Equal sharing does not always mean that every task must be divided exactly in half; both partners should contribute fairly.

In conclusion, I strongly agree that parents should share childcare and housework. This can reduce stress, improve family relationships and teach children the importance of cooperation.

# Essay 52 — Globalisation and National Identity

### Question
Globalisation has led to the loss of national identities. To what extent do you agree or disagree?

### Model answer

Globalisation has made it easier for people from different countries to communicate and share ideas. Although it can weaken some traditional customs, I partly agree that it can lead to a loss of national identity because people may become more influenced by foreign lifestyles.

One important reason is that people around the world increasingly watch similar films, use the same online platforms and buy products from international brands. When young people become more interested in foreign trends, they may pay less attention to their own traditions. For example, some young people may prefer international fashion to traditional clothing on important occasions. As a result, certain customs may become less common over time.

However, globalisation can also help countries share their cultures with the world. The internet allows people to introduce traditional food, music, festivals and crafts to international audiences. For example, visitors may learn about a country's culture through online videos and decide to experience it in person. This can encourage communities to protect and celebrate their traditions.

People can also enjoy foreign films and products while continuing to speak their native language and follow local customs. Therefore, globalisation does not automatically remove national identity.

In conclusion, I partly agree that globalisation can weaken traditional practices, particularly when people lose interest in their own culture. However, it can also promote cultural heritage if communities make an effort to preserve it.

# Essay 55 — Refugees from War-Torn Countries

### Question
Rich countries should accept more refugees from war-torn nations. To what extent do you agree or disagree?

### Model answer

Some people believe that wealthy countries should accept more refugees from nations affected by war. I largely agree because refugees often need safety and basic services, while richer countries may have more resources to support them.

One important reason is that people escaping war may lose their homes, jobs and access to education or healthcare. When families are forced to leave dangerous areas, they need a safe place to rebuild their lives. For example, children who escape a conflict may be able to continue their education if they are accepted into a safe country. As a result, providing protection can help vulnerable people recover and plan for the future.

Accepting refugees may also benefit host countries when newcomers are given opportunities to work and learn the local language. People who find employment can support themselves and contribute to the economy. However, governments must plan carefully because a rapid increase in population can put pressure on housing, schools and hospitals.

Wealthy countries should therefore work with local communities to provide suitable accommodation, language classes and access to employment. This can help refugees become independent while reducing pressure on public services.

In conclusion, I largely agree that rich countries should accept more refugees from war-torn nations. However, they should manage these programmes carefully so that refugees receive proper support and host communities can respond to their needs.

# Essay 58 — Tourism and Local Communities

### Question
Tourism has more negative impacts than positive effects on local communities. To what extent do you agree or disagree?

### Model answer

Tourism provides income for many communities, but it can also create environmental and social problems. I partly disagree that tourism has more negative effects than positive ones because it can create jobs and support local businesses when it is managed properly.

One important benefit is that tourism creates employment opportunities. Visitors need places to stay, food to eat and transport to reach attractions. When more tourists visit an area, local hotels, restaurants and shops may need additional workers. For example, a village that becomes a popular destination may create jobs for guides, drivers and restaurant staff. As a result, local people can earn an income and small businesses can grow.

However, tourism can also put pressure on local communities. Large numbers of visitors may create litter, traffic and noise, while popular destinations can experience rising prices. For example, an increase in demand for holiday accommodation may make housing more expensive for local residents. Tourism can also damage natural areas if visitors do not follow environmental rules.

Governments can reduce these problems by managing visitor numbers, improving waste collection and protecting natural and cultural sites. Local people should also receive a fair share of the income tourism generates.

In conclusion, I partly disagree that tourism has more negative effects than positive ones. Although it can create problems, good planning can help communities benefit from tourism while protecting their environment and way of life.

## Final practice checklist

Before submitting an IELTS essay, check:

- [ ] Did I answer every part of the question?
- [ ] Is my opinion clear in the introduction and conclusion?
- [ ] Does each body paragraph have one main idea?
- [ ] Did I explain each main idea and give a relevant example?
- [ ] Did I use complex sentences accurately, not just frequently?
- [ ] Did I check articles (*a, an, the*)?
- [ ] Did I check subject–verb agreement (*people are*, *a person is*)?
- [ ] Did I check singular and plural nouns?
- [ ] Did I check verb forms after *to*, *can*, *should* and *does*?
- [ ] Did I leave time to check spelling and punctuation?

**Important:** These models aim for an accessible Band 6.5 style, but no template or sample can guarantee a particular band. Your score depends on how accurately and fully you answer the question in the exam.
