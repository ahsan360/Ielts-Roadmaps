
# IELTS Writing Task 2 — Topic-Based Revision Guide

## How to Use This Guide

Memorise ideas, examples, and results rather than entire essays.

Use this structure to develop a body paragraph:

**Main idea → Explanation → Example → Result**

Example:
- **Idea:** Online learning saves time.
- **Explanation:** Students do not need to travel to educational institutions.
- **Example:** A student in a rural area can attend an online course from home.
- **Result:** Education becomes more accessible and convenient.

# 1. Art

**Main argument:** Art preserves cultural heritage, encourages creativity, and enriches people's lives. However, governments must balance cultural investment against essential public services.

| Topic | Simple idea | Example | Expected result |
|---|---|---|---|
| Traditional art | Young people prefer digital entertainment. | Teenagers spend more time on TikTok than visiting art exhibitions. | Traditional art becomes less popular. |
| Museums and galleries | Museums need interactive experiences. | A museum offers workshops and digital displays. | More young people visit. |
| Public art | Art improves public spaces. | A city installs sculptures in a public park. | The city becomes more attractive. |
| Art funding | Healthcare and education are more urgent. | A poor area needs a hospital more than an expensive sculpture. | Public money meets essential needs first. |

**Useful sentence:** Public investment in art can preserve cultural heritage; nevertheless, essential services should take priority when public funds are limited.

# 2. Communication and Technology

**Main argument:** Technology makes communication faster and more convenient, but excessive reliance on digital communication can weaken face-to-face relationships.

| Topic | Simple idea | Example | Expected result |
|---|---|---|---|
| Remote work | Employees save commuting time. | A software engineer works from home. | Lower travel costs and greater flexibility. |
| Face-to-face communication | Smartphones reduce direct interaction. | Friends look at their phones during dinner. | Personal relationships may weaken. |
| Social media news | News spreads rapidly, but misinformation does too. | Users share an unverified news story. | People may believe false information. |
| Fake news | Weak fact-checking allows misinformation to spread. | A platform labels suspicious posts. | Users become more cautious. |
| Modern communication | Digital tools maintain long-distance relationships. | Families use video calls to contact relatives abroad. | People remain emotionally connected. |

**Useful sentence:** Although digital platforms enable people to communicate instantly, excessive screen time can reduce meaningful face-to-face interaction.

# 3. Crime and Punishment

**Main argument:** Effective crime prevention requires both fair punishment and measures that address the underlying causes of criminal behaviour.

| Topic | Simple idea | Example | Expected result |
|---|---|---|---|
| Poverty and crime | Financial hardship can encourage theft. | An unemployed person steals to meet basic needs. | Employment opportunities may reduce some crimes. |
| Education and crime | Education develops skills and opportunities. | A young person receives vocational training. | The likelihood of offending may decrease. |
| Life imprisonment | It protects society from dangerous offenders. | A violent offender is imprisoned for life. | The public is protected from that individual. |
| Rehabilitation | Offenders can learn useful skills. | A prisoner receives counselling and job training. | Reintegration may become easier. |
| Capital punishment | Supporters argue that it deters serious crime. | A government introduces severe penalties for murder. | Some people may be discouraged, although deterrence is disputed. |
| Police firearms | Armed officers may respond to armed threats. | Police confront an armed attacker. | Officers may protect the public, but misuse risks remain. |

**Useful sentence:** While strict punishment can protect society, rehabilitation may reduce reoffending by addressing the factors that contribute to criminal behaviour.

**Remember:** For capital punishment essays, consider deterrence, human rights, wrongful convictions, and the possibility of rehabilitation.

| Topic | Simple idea | Example | Expected result |

# 12. Essay-Type Strategy

## A. Discussion + Opinion

**Structure**
1. Introduction: Paraphrase the question and state your opinion.
2. Body paragraph 1: Explain the first viewpoint.
3. Body paragraph 2: Explain the second viewpoint and your preferred position.
4. Conclusion: Summarise both views and reaffirm your opinion.

**Useful phrases**
- On the one hand, ...
- On the other hand, ...
- Although both arguments have merit, I believe that ...
- In conclusion, while ..., I would argue that ...

## B. Agree or Disagree

**Structure**
1. Introduction: Paraphrase the statement and give a clear position.
2. Body paragraph 1: Explain your first reason.
3. Body paragraph 2: Explain your second reason.
4. Conclusion: Restate your position.

**Useful phrases**
- I largely agree with this view because ...
- One compelling reason is that ...
- Furthermore, ...
- For these reasons, I believe that ...

## C. Causes and Solutions

**Structure**
1. Introduction: Introduce the problem.
2. Body paragraph 1: Explain the main causes.
3. Body paragraph 2: Propose practical solutions.
4. Conclusion: Summarise the causes and measures.

**Useful phrases**
- One major contributing factor is ...
- This problem is partly attributable to ...
- To address this issue, governments should ...
- These measures could help to ...

## D. Advantages and Disadvantages

**Structure**
1. Introduction: Paraphrase the development.
2. Body paragraph 1: Explain the advantages.
3. Body paragraph 2: Explain the disadvantages.
4. Conclusion: Give a balanced judgement if the question asks whether one side outweighs the other.

**Useful phrases**
- One significant advantage is that ...
- Another benefit is ...
- Nevertheless, this development also has drawbacks.
- On balance, I believe the advantages outweigh the disadvantages because ...

## E. Two-Part / Mixed Questions

**Structure**
1. Introduction: Briefly introduce the issue and, if appropriate, answer both questions.
2. Body paragraph 1: Answer the first question.
3. Body paragraph 2: Answer the second question.
4. Conclusion: Summarise both answers.

**Useful phrases**
- There are several reasons why this occurs.
- One important explanation is that ...
- Regarding its effects, ...
- Overall, this trend can be attributed to ..., and its main consequence is ...

# Final Revision Checklist

- [ ] State a clear position when the question asks for an opinion.
- [ ] Answer every part of the question.
- [ ] Explain each idea instead of merely listing it.
- [ ] Include a relevant, realistic example.
- [ ] Show the consequence of the example or argument.
- [ ] Use linking words naturally rather than excessively.
- [ ] Check articles, subject–verb agreement, verb tense, and sentence structure.
- [ ] Aim for at least 250 words in Writing Task 2.

**Remember:** Clear ideas, logical development, relevant examples, and accurate grammar are more valuable than unnecessarily complicated vocabulary.
